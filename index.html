Somebody Else Is Holding Your Money Right Now.

Your pay before it reaches your account. The deposit on a house, sitting with a lawyer. The rent you sent last week. Money you own spends a lot of its life in somebody else's bank account, and the law is strict about what that somebody has to be able to prove. This site explains that duty in plain words, why it is hard to meet, and what we are building for it.

Five steps, and every one of them is somebody's job.

This is the whole idea. If you follow these five lines you understand what this company is for.

1

Money comes in

A customer's pay, a deposit on a house, a month's rent, money collected on somebody's behalf.

2

It is not the firm's money

The firm is only holding it. It belongs to the person who sent it or the person waiting for it.

3

It has to be kept apart

In its own account, not mixed with the money the firm runs on. That is what a trust account is.

4

It has to be checked on a clock

What the firm says it holds for each person, against what the bank says is actually there. Every day for some firms, every month for others.

5

It has to be produced later

An inspector, an auditor or a court asks for a specific day, sometimes years afterwards, and the firm has to hand it over.

The whole difficulty is step five. Doing the work is one thing. Proving you did it, on a day two years ago, is another. Most firms can do the first. The second is where the trouble is.

What happens if you get it wrong

The ceiling went up by up to forty times.

A ceiling is the most a regulator may impose. Until March 2026 the money laundering ceiling for a company was five hundred thousand dollars. It is now twenty million, and for a single notice covering several breaches it can instead be three per cent of the company's worldwide revenue, whichever of the two is greater.

The money laundering figures come from section 73.1 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, as amended by Bill C-12, which received Royal Assent and came into force on 26 March 2026. The Act sets the maximum for one prescribed violation at four million dollars for a person and twenty million for a company. Where one notice covers several violations, the cap is the greater of that amount and three per cent of gross global income or revenue. The regulator has described the change as raising limits up to forty times. All bars share one scale.

These are not theoretical numbers. In October 2025 the money laundering regulator imposed a single penalty of $176,960,190 on one company. That penalty is recorded as under appeal, so it is not a final figure, but it shows the size the regime now operates at. In its 2024 to 2025 financial year the same regulator issued twenty three notices of violation totalling more than twenty five million dollars.

Losing the registration is the other consequence. For most of these businesses it is the heavier one. A fine can be argued about. A cancelled registration means the firm stops trading.

Who this is about

If your firm holds money for other people, this is your duty and not your bank's.

The duty attaches to the firm holding the money. No bank, processor or sponsor carries it for you. Here are six of the ten kinds of firm this covers.

Daily · Bank of Canada

Payment firms

Payroll companies, money transfer apps, gift card businesses. Anyone holding or moving money for other people without being a bank.

Monthly · law societies

Law firms

A lawyer holding your house deposit is the everyday example. The money is yours the whole time it sits there.

Monthly · federal

Insolvency trustees

When a person or a company goes bankrupt, the trustee holds money that is owed to the people who are waiting to be paid.

Six years · Ontario

Mortgage brokerages

Money paid in advance on a mortgage is treated by law as money held in trust from the moment it arrives.

Monthly · provincial

Property managers

Rent and damage deposits belong to landlords and tenants. The manager is holding them, unit by unit.

Two banking days · Ontario

Collection agencies

Money collected from a debtor belongs to the creditor. It has to reach a trust account almost immediately.
