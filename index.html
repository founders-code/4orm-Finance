How the duty works

Five steps. Four of them are easy.

This is the shape of the obligation, whichever regulator you answer to.

1

Money arrives

A client deposit, an employee’s pay, a month’s rent, a payment collected for a creditor.

2

It stays theirs

You are the custodian. The money belongs to the sender or the person waiting for it the entire time you hold it.

3

It sits apart

In its own account, identified to the person it belongs to. That is a trust account.

4

It gets checked

Your list against the bank’s. Daily for payment firms, monthly for law firms, trustees and property managers.

5

It gets produced

An examiner, an auditor or a court names a date. You hand over that day’s record, and the working behind it.

Step five is the expensive one. Most firms handle the first four. The fifth is where the weeks go. Reconstructing proof for a date two years back means going through bank exports, ledgers, spreadsheets and email, and hoping the person who did the work still remembers it.

What a breach costs

The ceiling rose by up to 40 times in March 2026.

A ceiling is the maximum a regulator may impose. Until 26 March 2026 the money laundering maximum for a company was $500,000. It is now $20,000,000, or 3 per cent of worldwide revenue where one notice covers several violations, whichever is greater.

In October 2025 FINTRAC assessed $176,960,190 against Xeltox Enterprises Ltd. It is the largest penalty in Canadian history and it is under appeal. In its 2024 to 2025 financial year FINTRAC issued 23 notices of violation totalling more than $25 million. A cancelled registration ends the business outright, which for most firms is the heavier outcome.

Who carries it

The duty sits with the firm holding the money.

No bank, processor or sponsor carries it for you. Six of the ten kinds of firm this covers.

Daily · Bank of Canada

Payment firms

Payroll bureaus, money transfer apps, gift card issuers. Any business holding or moving money for other people without being a bank.

Monthly · law societies

Law firms

House deposits, settlement funds and retainers sit in the firm’s trust account. The money belongs to the client throughout.

Monthly · federal

Insolvency trustees

Each bankruptcy is a separate pot of money owed to creditors waiting to be paid.

6 years · Ontario

Mortgage brokerages

An advance payment on a mortgage becomes trust money the moment it arrives.

Monthly · provincial

Property managers

Rent belongs to the landlord. Damage deposits belong to the tenant and have to come back. Both are tracked unit by unit.

2 banking days · Ontario

Collection agencies

Money collected from a debtor belongs to the creditor and has to reach a trust account almost immediately.
