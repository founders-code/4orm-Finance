The rules

The Rules, In Plain Words.

Nothing on this page is our opinion. Every date, deadline and dollar figure below comes from a law, a regulation or a professional rule, and each one is named so you can read it yourself. Plain wording is a summary. The instrument itself is what governs.

Words you will meet

Nine words, and then no more jargon.

These are the only special terms on this site. Everything after this uses ordinary English.

The money words

What the money is called.

Trust accountA bank account holding money that belongs to somebody else
SafeguardingKeeping customer money apart from the firm's own, and being able to prove it
ReconciliationChecking two lists against each other, what the firm says it holds against what the bank says is there
ShortfallWhen the two lists do not agree and there is less money than there should be
The rule words

What the officials are called.

RegulatorThe government body that writes the rules and checks that a firm follows them
RegistrationGovernment permission to operate. A firm applies and the regulator decides
Penalty ceilingThe most a regulator may fine a firm for one breach
Independent reviewAn outside firm checking the records and signing the result
Retention periodHow long the proof has to be kept before it can be thrown away
The clock that already started

This is not a rule that is coming. It arrived in September 2025.

Three dates matter. One has passed, one comes round every year, and one is waiting at the end.

8 Sep 2025
The Bank of Canada began supervising payment firms. The safeguarding requirements came into force on this day.
31 Mar, yearly
Every registered payment firm files an annual report. It is due by 31 March for the year before.
8 Sep 2028
The first independent review falls due. It reads the three years of records before it, which means the records being made today.
The 2028 date is the one worth sitting with. A review that looks back three years is reading records that are being created now, and a record nobody kept cannot be made later at any price.
The fines

What a regulator may impose, and who sets each one.

Every figure below is a ceiling for a single breach. Ceilings are not typical fines. They are published so that firms know the outer limit of what is at stake.

CeilingWhat it coversSet bySince
$1,000,000A serious breach of the payment rulesRetail Payment Activities Act8 Sep 2025
$4,000,000One money laundering violation, by a personProceeds of Crime Act, section 73.1(2)26 Mar 2026
$10,000,000A very serious breach of the payment rulesRetail Payment Activities Act8 Sep 2025
$20,000,000One money laundering violation, by a companyProceeds of Crime Act, section 73.1(2)26 Mar 2026
3% of revenueWhere one notice covers several violations, the cap is the greater of the amounts above and three per cent of gross global income or revenueProceeds of Crime Act, section 73.1(3)26 Mar 2026

Before 26 March 2026 the money laundering ceiling for a company was five hundred thousand dollars for one very serious violation, and one hundred thousand for an individual. Bill C-12 received Royal Assent on 26 March 2026 and the new limits took effect the same day. The regulator has described the change as raising limits up to forty times.

One real example, so the numbers are not abstract. In October 2025 a single company was assessed a penalty of $176,960,190. It is recorded as under appeal, so treat it as an amount imposed rather than an amount collected. In the same regulator's 2024 to 2025 financial year it issued twenty three notices of violation totalling more than twenty five million dollars.

The other clocks

Money laundering rules run on their own timetable.

A firm can be under the payment rules and the money laundering rules at once, watched by two different bodies, on two different schedules. These are the deadlines that catch people out.

WhatWhen it is dueNamed in
A report of a suspicious transactionAs soon as practicable after you have finished the checks that let you conclude there are reasonable grounds to suspectFINTRAC reporting guidance
A report of a large cash transactionWithin 15 calendar days after the day the cash was receivedFINTRAC reporting guidance
A report of terrorist propertyImmediately, meaning no delay before submittingFINTRAC reporting guidance
A review of whether your compliance programme worksEvery two years at a minimum, starting no later than 24 months after the last one beganFINTRAC compliance programme requirements

Notice that only one of these four is a fixed number of days. The rest are triggered by something happening, which means the clock starts without anybody sending you a reminder.

One row is worth the whole table. A payment firm owes a record for every customer at the end of every day. That is three hundred and sixty five records a year for each customer, and any one of them can be asked for eighteen months later.
