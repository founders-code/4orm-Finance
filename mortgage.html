What the professional owes

A file another broker could re-perform.

Ontario's regulator expects mortgage documentation to tell the story of how a recommendation was suitable for that specific client, in enough detail that a third party could redo the assessment.

How it works

It starts before you have a broker, and it never stops recording.

How it behaves

The rules the product holds itself to.

Permissions are the consumer's
Each organization's access is granted, scoped and withdrawable, with the timestamps kept.
Answers carry their evidence
Every transaction-specific sentence exposes the document or version it came from.
Uncertainty is shown
Extracted figures stay proposed until the consumer confirms them. A recollection is labelled as one.
Conflicts are stated
Where two sources disagree, both are shown. The product does not pick a side or assign blame.
History is not rewritten
Withdrawing a permission stops future use. It does not erase what was lawfully done before it.

Mortgage Guardian gives educational guidance and shows you your own record. It is not a mortgage approval, it is not a regulated product recommendation, and 4orm is not a mortgage brokerage, a lender, a law firm or a regulator.

Why it matters

The requirement already exists. The record to satisfy it usually does not.

Three published sources shape what this product has to be. Each one is linked.

FSRA · Ontario

Show your work

Suitability documentation is expected to tell a clear story of how a recommendation fits the specific client, sufficient for another broker to re-perform the assessment.

What it means here: rationale and decision history are preserved as data, not only documents. Read the guidance →
FINTRAC · Federal

Compliance and recordkeeping

Covered mortgage administrators, brokers and lenders carry compliance-program, identity, monitoring, recordkeeping and reporting obligations.

What it means here: identity, business relationship and evidence events are built to extend into those workflows. Read the obligations →
Bank of Canada

Consumer-driven banking

Canada is implementing a framework for secure, consumer-directed sharing of financial data with approved providers.

What it means here: permissions and provenance are core architecture, even while the demo runs on synthetic data. Read the framework →
