Know what is happening with your mortgage.

The consumer prepares before they choose anybody. The broker receives a prepared client and a rationale recorded as the work happens. The brokerage supervises by exception. And every one of them is looking at the same record.

The challenge

One transaction split across six organizations and nobody holding the evidence of it.

Your mortgage moves through six organizations. The record of it moves through none of them intact.

What the consumer sees

A request, then silence

You send documents into an email thread and wait. When a number on your file changes, you find out at the end, if at all.

What the professional owes

A file another broker could re-perform

Ontario expects mortgage documentation to tell the story of how a recommendation was suitable for that specific client, in enough detail that a third party could redo the assessment.

What nobody holds

The transaction itself

A broker, a brokerage, one or more lenders, an insurer, a lawyer and the consumer’s own inbox. Each holds a piece. None holds the story.

What it catches

An income figure moved by $18,000. No document moved with it.

Every figure in this example is invented. The shape of it is not.

01

A lender declines

On 5 April, at the stated income of $118,000.

02

The next day the figure changes

Annual income becomes $136,000 on the application. No supporting document is attached to the change.

03

A second lender approves on the new figure

A conditional approval is issued on 14 April, relying on a number the evidence on file does not support.

04

Both sides are told, the same minute

The consumer sees what changed and what their own documents support. The brokerage sees an evidence exception. Neither is told who is at fault.

4orm does not accuse anyone. It states the change, shows what the evidence supports, and asks the one person who can settle it. An anomaly is a question for the people on the file, not a finding against one of them.
