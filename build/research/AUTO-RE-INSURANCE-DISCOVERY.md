# Discovery: auto, real estate, insurance

Written 2026-09-02, the same way the mortgage set was written. Every
question in the phone traces to a line in this document, and every line
traces to a regulator, a regulator's own examination findings, or a
published figure. Nothing here was invented to sound useful.

The rule that produced the mortgage set applies unchanged: **the
discovery is fixed, not generated.** A suitability file has to be
re-performable by a third party. A conversation that asks a different
question every time cannot be re-performed, so the questions stay
deterministic and the explaining stays on the seams.

---

## Why these three, and why in this order

Each of the three has one moment where the person loses the ability to
undo the decision, and in each case it arrives earlier than people
expect.

| Decision | The point of no return | How early it arrives |
|---|---|---|
| Auto | Signing the bill of sale | Ontario has **no cooling-off period** on a vehicle purchase. There is no day-after |
| Real estate | Signing the representation agreement | Before a single property has been seen, and it can bind you after it expires |
| Insurance | The policy being bound, or an existing one replaced | Replacing a policy you already hold can be irreversible if your health has changed since you bought it |

So all three discoveries are built to run **before** that moment, and
each opener is written to catch the person who is already past it.

---

## 1. Auto

### The regulator

Ontario dealers are registered with **OMVIC** under the Motor Vehicle
Dealers Act. What OMVIC requires of them is what the person is entitled
to ask for.

### All-in price advertising

The advertised price must include **every fee the dealer intends to
collect**, except HST and licensing, and the ad must say plainly that
those two are excluded. In scope:

- freight and pre-delivery inspection
- administration fees and the OMVIC transaction fee
- government levies, including air tax, the Ontario green levy and luxury tax
- safety certification, unless the vehicle is sold as-is or unfit and the ad says so
- anything pre-installed: nitrogen, warranties, security etching, fuel

Every fee must then be **itemised separately on the bill of sale**.
OMVIC's own instruction if fees appear that were not in the advertised
price: walk away, shop elsewhere, and report it.

This produces one question and one action, not a lecture.

### Negative equity

OMVIC defines it as owing more than the vehicle is worth, and names two
things that cause it: **longer terms and higher rates**. FCAC found
**30% of consumers trading in a vehicle were in negative equity**, up
from 20% five years earlier, and describes rolling the shortfall into
the next loan as an "auto-debt treadmill".

OMVIC's chief consumer protection officer states the test plainly: do
not borrow more than you can repay **over the entire term**, not just
this month and next.

That is the question. It is also the reason the question about the
trade-in comes before the question about the payment.

### What the numbers actually are

| Figure | Value | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Average new-car loan | $54,493 over 72 months at 6.45%, $914.73 a month | Jan 2026 |
| Average used-car loan | $31,306 over 60 months at 6.45%, $611.80 a month | Jan 2026 |
| Share of institutional portfolios at 72+ months | about 60% | FCAC |
| First-year depreciation | 15% to 25% | Canadian Black Book |
| Subprime auto rates | from about 12%, reaching the 20s and 30s | OMVIC |

### The question set

| # | Question | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | How far into this are you | The point of no return is signing, and someone standing at the desk needs a different answer from someone browsing |
| A2 | Is there a vehicle you are trading, and do you still owe on it | Negative equity. FCAC's 30%. This has to come before the payment question, because it is what makes the payment misleading |
| A3 | How is it being paid for | Dealer financing, own bank, cash or lease change what is being decided and who is deciding it |
| A4 | The nosy one: what would you be comfortable paying, and for how long | OMVIC's "over the entire term" test. The monthly payment is the number the desk works in, and it is the wrong number |
| A5 | What matters most | Lowest payment, lowest total, owning it outright, being able to change in three years. Naming the trade is what stops it being made for you |

## 2. Real estate

### The regulator

**RECO**, under **TRESA**, which changed on 1 December 2023 and changed
what a consumer is entitled to before anybody starts working for them.

### What TRESA actually gave people

- The **RECO Information Guide** has to be given and acknowledged
  **before any services are provided**. Not at the offer. Before.
- **Customer service agreements no longer exist.** You are a client, or
  you are a **self-represented party**, and a self-represented party is
  owed no duty of care. There is no longer a middle.
- **Designated representation**: one brokerage can act for both sides
  with a different agent designated to each, each owing their duty to
  their own client. **Multiple representation**, one agent for both,
  needs written consent from everybody.
- Every disclosure must be **in writing, in clear and concise language,
  labelled as a disclosure**, signed, and a copy handed back.
- A seller may now authorise their brokerage to **disclose the details
  of competing offers**, excluding personal identifying information.

### The holdover clause

The part that catches people. A holdover extends the brokerage's
commission entitlement **after the agreement has ended**, commonly 30
days, sometimes far longer. It is **not required by anything**. Term
length, commission and holdover are all **negotiable**, and a buyer can
ask for the holdover to be shortened or removed.

A worked case: a 90-day agreement expires, the buyer closes 20 days
later on a property the agent had shown, and commission is still owed.
On a mid-priced Ontario home that is $15,000 or more.

Registrants are searchable on RECO's public register, licence and
discipline history included.

### The question set

| # | Question | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Which side of this are you on | Selling, buying, or both. Duties differ, and "both" is where multiple representation appears |
| R2 | Have you signed anything yet | The single most consequential fact, and it decides whether this conversation is preparation or triage |
| R3 | Do you know how long it runs, and what happens after it ends | Term and holdover, the two negotiable numbers almost nobody negotiates |
| R4 | The nosy one: has anyone explained what happens if the same brokerage ends up on both sides | Designated versus multiple representation, and the written consent that is supposed to precede it |
| R5 | What matters most | Price, speed, certainty of closing, or not being tied up. This is what a representation agreement should have been shaped around |

## 3. Insurance

### The regulator

**FSRA**, and unusually we do not have to infer what goes wrong, because
FSRA published what it found when it examined agents.

### What FSRA found

319 life agents examined across 2022-23 and 2023-24. **35%, being 112
agents, resulted in regulatory action.** The recurring best-practice
failures, by count of instances:

| Failure | 2022-23 | 2023-24 |
|---|---|---|
| Contemporaneous notes missing | 124 | 111 |
| **Disclosure of the range of products offered** | 111 | 110 |
| Delivery receipt missing | 97 | 92 |
| **Reason-why letter missing** | 64 | 72 |
| **Insurance needs analysis missing** | 59 | 46 |

The three in bold are the ones a person can check for themselves, and
they become questions directly:

- *Did they tell you which companies they can actually sell from?*
- *Did you get anything in writing saying why this one?*
- *Did anybody work out how much you need before recommending an amount?*

A person who can ask those three has closed the gap that produced most
of 941 and 1,022 recorded issues.

### Replacement

Being moved off a policy you already hold is the highest-risk
transaction in the file, because the old policy was underwritten when
you were younger and healthier, and it does not come back. Replacement
carries its own declaration and disclosure requirements, which is
exactly why the opener names it as its own door.

### The question set

| # | Question | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | What is this about | Someone recommended something, replacing what I have, reviewing it, or starting from nothing. Replacement gets its own door because it is the irreversible one |
| I2 | Who would be affected if something happened to you | The needs analysis FSRA found missing 59 and 46 times. It is the first question, and often it is never asked |
| I3 | What is already in place | Group cover through work, an existing policy, or nothing. You cannot size what is needed without it |
| I4 | The nosy one: how long could the household manage on what is there now | Turns an abstract amount into a number of months, which is the only form of it anyone can actually judge |
| I5 | Did they say which companies they can sell from | 111 and 110 instances. The single most repeated failure, and a question anyone can ask in eight words |

## What this does not do

None of the three tells a person which vehicle, which agent, or which
policy to take, and none of them is advice. Each produces the same
artefact the mortgage discovery produces: **the person's own situation,
in their own words, written down before anybody is selling**, plus a
short list of things to find out that they can act on alone.

The four excluded elicitation techniques stay excluded here as they are
everywhere else: no flattery to deflect, no complaint baiting, no
provocative statements, no presumptive statements.
