# The home page brief
Governing direction, in the user's own words. This outranks my judgment on this page.

## The four surfaces, and the different job each one does

| Surface | Job | It says |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing** `/` | Emotion, curiosity, choice | Where do you want to go? |
| **App experience** (the phone) | Use the product | Experience 4orm. |
| **Firm experience** (the dashboard) | See the business value | See what 4orm gives the organization. |
| **Home page** `/home` | Understand the company | What is this? Why does it matter? Why now? Why should I trust you? |

The home page is **not** another version of the landing page, and **not** a directory of every
page on the site. It must work equally well for a consumer, a professional, a firm executive or
an investor **without asking them to pick a persona first**.

It should answer in about sixty seconds: what is 4orm, why does it need to exist, why should I
trust it, and what should I do next.

## Hard direction
- Completely **relationship-first**. Drop the older three-parties / regulator framing and the
  "intelligence and evidence layer" line.
- Large typography, exceptional whitespace, **one dominant visual idea per viewport**.
- **No SaaS card grid.** No feature dumps. Not 30 sections.
- The **single thin 4orm line** runs through the whole page as the relationship, evolving from
  connection into permission path, information flow, and finally an evidence timeline.
- The phone appears **selectively** and can be picked up again to relaunch product mode.
- **Do not lead with** AI, compliance, regulators, penalties, KYC, dashboards or evidence
  infrastructure. Those are revealed progressively, as proof of what sits beneath the relationship.

## The nav on the home page
`4orm Finance` on the left — clicking it returns to the landing page.
Then `Personal`, `Professional`, `4orm Family` in normal weight.
Then the blue pill: `4orm your experience` — opens the white directory menu.
"About" becomes **4orm Family**, and it goes to the team.

## The story, in order

The home page may run longer than three screens. It is the one page on the site allowed to.

**01 Thesis.** White. Huge type. Very little else.
"Better financial decisions start with better relationships."
Then: "4orm helps people understand important financial decisions while giving the firms serving
them a clearer, more defensible record of the relationship."
Then the two sides — FOR YOU: Know before you decide. FOR YOUR FIRM: Protect the relationship.
Prove the work. Buttons: Experience 4orm · For firms. That is enough.

**02 The challenge.** (Never the word "problem" — the gate bans it, and "the pain" is the honest word.) "Financial decisions aren't happening in one place."
Words scattered and disconnected across a near-empty page: Conversation, Email, Documents,
Broker, Application, Lender, Credit, Consent, Text message, Disclosure, Decision.
Then: "Important financial decisions move through conversations, people, documents and systems
that were never designed to preserve the complete relationship."
Then the thin 4orm line moves through them. "That's where things get lost."
Show the pain. Do not explain it in three paragraphs.

**03 The data.** "The numbers tell the story." Three or four giant numbers, one at a time on
scroll. No little cards. $704M+ · 47% · 100% · 100%. Then: "Consumers need clarity. Firms need
evidence. Both need a better relationship." Link out to the research rather than becoming a
research report.

**04 Then show 4orm.** "One relationship. One connected record."
YOU on the left, YOUR FIRM on the right, the thin blue line between them. Events appear on it as
the visitor scrolls: Question, Information, Verification, Permission, Professional,
Recommendation, Change, Decision, Evidence.
Then: "4orm helps preserve the context between these moments — not simply the documents created
at the end." That's the product.

**05 The consumer side.** "Know before you decide." The phone beside it, showing Mortgage, Auto,
Insurance, Lending. "Ask questions. Prepare. Verify information. Understand what you're sharing.
Keep track of what changes." Then, critically: **Your information. Your decision.**
Buttons: Pick up 4orm (relaunches the black phone experience) · Learn about Personal.

**06 Flip the same relationship around.** The phone moves left, the line continues, a
professional workspace emerges on the right. "Better prepared clients. Better professional
relationships." One transaction, not a giant dashboard: Sarah Mitchell, identity verified, income
document supported, permissions current, questions resolved, needs attention 1. Then the one
item: income doesn't match, $120,000 against $138,000, with Explain / Correct / Request evidence.
Then: "Protect the relationship before it becomes a problem."

**07 Show the executive why they pay.** "Know how your clients are being served." Very sparse:
148 active relationships, 141 on track, 7 need attention. Examples of what needs attention. Then:
"4orm helps firms identify material relationship gaps while there is still time to address them."
And: **"Not employee surveillance. Relationship integrity."** That line matters.

**08 The evidence reveal.** Black section, late. The thin blue line becomes a timeline. Every
event now has a source and a timestamp. "The evidence was being created the whole time."
Then: the consumer experienced guidance, the professional experienced a better-prepared client,
the firm received a clearer record, 4orm preserved what mattered between them.
And: **"One honest interaction. One defensible record."**

**09 Then let the regulators speak.** Only now. "The standard is moving toward proof."
Three pieces of authoritative language in large quotation treatment: "Tell a clear story." ·
"Show your work." · "Re-performance." Beneath: the expectation increasingly isn't simply that a
process happened. Link to The Standard.

**10 What 4orm is not.** Very Apple. Black text, white screen. "We don't replace the
relationship." Then four lines in sequence: Not your mortgage broker. Not your dealer. Not your
lender. Not the regulator. Then: "4orm helps make the relationship clearer." Small print on
professional judgment remaining with the professional.

**11 Privacy.** Emotional treatment, not a security card wall. Black screen, small mark.
**"Your financial life isn't ours."** See what you have shared. Know why information is being
requested. Control optional permissions where applicable. Correct information that's wrong.
Understand where information came from. Keep sensitive information protected.
Then the honest caveat: some participating firms may have independent legal obligations to
retain records after information has been shared with them. That is more credible than "your
data, your rules", because legally that is not universally true.

**12 Breadth, without diluting the story.** "Starting with the financial decisions that matter
most." Mortgage — experience available. Auto — experience available. Insurance — expanding.
Lending — expanding. Then: "Different decisions. The same need for understanding, permission,
provenance and evidence."

**13 Integrations.** "Built to work with the financial system." 4orm at the centre; around it
Identity, Financial data, Credit, Documents, Professional systems, Authoritative sources,
Reporting. "4orm doesn't need to replace the systems firms already use. It connects the important
evidence around them." No fake logos. No "partners" unless they actually are.

**14 The close.** Everything strips away. Massive whitespace. "Better financial decisions start
with better relationships." Two choices: I'M MAKING A DECISION → Experience 4orm · I SERVE
CLIENTS → See 4orm for firms. Then: Talk to 4orm.

## The test
The landing page captures attention. The experiences make them say "I get it."
The home page should make them say: **"Now I understand why this company needs to exist."**
