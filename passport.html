Why it reads this way

A number that looks like an approval would be one, and 4orm is not a lender. A preparation score says something different and more useful: here is what a broker or a lender is going to ask you for, here is what you already have, and here is what is missing.

Identification, employment and income, down payment and assets, and debt information are what a mortgage professional needs at preapproval. The score is measured against that list and nothing else.

Every item links to the document behind it, and every document shows what was read from it and whether you have confirmed it.

What the consumer downloads

Nobody wants compliance evidence software. People do want to understand a large financial decision before they make it, to know who holds their information, and to be told when something changes.

What the transaction gains

The evidence, built as a by-product.

  1. Identity and verification, captured once and reusable.
  2. A permission ledger showing who received what, and when.
  3. Provenance on every value, from where it was stated to where it was relied on.
  4. A decision history that survives staff changes on either side.
  5. A transaction record another professional could re-perform.
One journey, two products. Every question answered, document uploaded and permission granted does both jobs at once. That is the whole design.
What it is not

Five limits, so nobody is surprised later.

Limits

The boundary.

  1. 4orm is not a mortgage brokerage, a lender, a law firm or a regulator.
  2. It gives educational guidance and shows you your own record. It does not give regulated advice about which mortgage product to take.
  3. A readiness score is a preparation measure. It is not a credit decision, an approval, or an indication of how much you can borrow.
  4. Where two sources disagree, both are shown. 4orm does not decide who is right and does not allege that anybody did anything wrong.
  5. Withdrawing a permission stops future use. It does not erase what was lawfully done before it.
